Several inherited claims must not drive decisions:

- **The constant is no longer the incumbent.** “Nothing beats 14.5%” predates the checkpoint-selection fix and mixes validation and test numbers. Compare candidates with the corrected CNN on the same validation images.
- **Paired IDs do not establish repeated measurements of one cornea.** A fresh ID-only check found 2,225 of 2,227 two-image groups consist of an `ODCN` and an `OSCN` image. These are fellow-eye naming patterns, not evidence of repeated annotation of the same tissue. Their differences cannot establish an irreducible label-noise floor or justify freezing CV/HEX to constants.
- **The 25 overlays are not all training data.** The split check gives 19 train, two validation (`0334-23`, `0587-23`), and four test (`0324-23`, `0329-23`, `0471-23`, `0586-23`). Use only train overlays for fitting, dot extraction decisions, ROI rules, or calibration. The small annotated cohort cannot establish generalization across eye banks.
- **Some recovered probes already examined test labels and predictions.** Those numbers remain exploratory historical evidence; do not adopt their winning settings or present them as independent confirmation. The four exact train/test duplicate pairs documented in the audit are an additional limitation. A later frozen test report must disclose both prior access and duplicates.
- **The label operator remains a hypothesis.** The overlays suggest contiguous center annotation in a selected region. Their rasterized dots do not identify the exact vendor algorithm. A good score using human dots grants the detector both locations and region selection; it is not an end-to-end model result. Baseline fitted-hexagon IoU and a six-neighbor fraction are distinct quantities and must have distinct artifact names.
- **No universal shortcut converts correlation into MAPE.** Evaluate actual predictions. Geometric averaging, log loss, shrinkage, and clipping are candidates, not mathematical guarantees of improvement. For positive targets, minimizing MAPE is a target-weighted absolute-error problem; log-L1 is a different objective.

**Protocol and promotion rules**

Keep the existing 7,202 / 892 / 906 train/validation/test partitions and cached image order. Fit on train, select the bounded candidate choices on validation, and leave test unused during development. Within train, group cross-validation by the existing `slide_id`, keeping any known duplicate components together. This is a grouping safeguard, not proof of donor independence. Do not rebuild the existing 9,000-image diameter-40 mask cache.

Every candidate must save predictions keyed by `ID`, full expected-ID coverage, CD/CV/HEX errors, mean error, exact configuration, model checksum, and timing. Use original labels for scoring. The current local metric skips near-zero targets separately for each metric (`abs(target) <= 1e-8`); retain that behavior and report counts. The public formula does not specify zero handling, so confirm it before claiming exact evaluator equivalence. Do not remove an entire image because one metric is zero, invent an epsilon that changes ranking, or clamp CV to one: CV is not a probability.

For learned postprocessing, generate predictions out of fold **within train**. A row's model fitting, normalization, early stopping, calibration-feature selection, and pseudo-label supervision must exclude its outer fold. Fit calibration or stacking on these predictions; evaluate the resulting complete pipeline on untouched validation predictions. Do not fit a calibrator on validation and report its training error as validation performance. If full out-of-fold training costs too much, use a fixed grouped holdback within train for calibration and accept the smaller fitting set. In-sample outputs from an overfit base learner are not an adequate substitute.

Use a paired slide-clustered bootstrap on validation error differences, preserving the original image-weighted score within each resample. Report per-metric counts and differences as well as the mean. If simulating 100-image evaluations, state the sampling assumptions and include cohort-mix sensitivity; this is not the probability of advancing. Inspect the upper error quantile, not the lower, when measuring adverse outcomes. Repeated validation selection also makes nominal intervals optimistic; the bounded experiment list below limits this exposure.

Predeclare these practical gates before implementation:

- **Core model replacement:** seek at least **0.3 percentage points** lower mean validation error than the incumbent, a consistent direction across at least two of three paired seeds for small-CNN changes, and a paired 95% bootstrap interval favoring the candidate. Examine each metric and large-error cases. An uncertain improvement remains provisional; do not claim it is established.
- **Added component:** a feature stream, second model, calibration layer, or TTA must improve the *complete pipeline* by at least **0.2 points**, with a paired interval supporting the direction, and pass the same runtime and parity checks. A weaker standalone model may qualify through complementary errors. Do not require every metric to improve if the equal-weight mean clearly improves; document the tradeoff.
- **Tie:** keep the simpler, already verified pipeline. These thresholds are engineering choices, not universal statistical resolution limits. If measured seed variation or intervals are larger, stop rather than adding a search to chase the threshold.
- **Failure:** enforce a time cap even when a branch is promising. Never block the verified submission on an unfinished optional experiment. A good local score without a deployable model does not pass.

**How the literature affects the choice**

The recovered literature scout overstates what published methods establish about this dataset. Doughty's center-dot comparison found similar mean area but inflated area CV, associated with an erroneous large cell domain; a follow-up reported substantial agreement limits despite similar averages. This supports testing readout sensitivity, not declaring a universal factor-of-two correction or asserting that the exact CLEAR-EC boundary rule is known. [Center-dot comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18045256/), [follow-up study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18537989/)

Published automated endothelial analysis also uses different cohorts, instruments and reference annotations. For example, Vigueras-Guillén and colleagues studied 383 postoperative images from 41 eyes using a Topcon SP-1P. Such work supports the plausibility of image-based estimation; it cannot establish a winning CLEAR-EC error threshold. The recovered scout mixes author attributions and percentage units, so its numerical literature ranking targets are not carried into this plan.
